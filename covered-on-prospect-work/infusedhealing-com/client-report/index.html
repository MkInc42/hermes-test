Client Presence Review

for Infused Healing

Infused Healing has a distinctive, caring offer, but its public presence makes a few important trust and discovery details harder to follow than they need to be.

Top action: Correct the ceremony page links and next-step language first, then use that page as the model for a clearer visitor path across the site.

Public-information scope: We reviewed what a new visitor can see. We did not log in, submit forms, test bookings or checkout, contact the business, access private systems, or review customer data.

Prioritized path

Six opportunities

Each action answers one or more findings above. Impact, effort, and confidence are practical planning labels, not outcome promises.

1Now
Answers F Trust 1 and F Conversion 1, source S6

Fix the ceremony page before sending more visitors to it

Observation: The page has a focused message and intake form, but its inline social icons lead to default Wix accounts.

Why it matters: A trust check can take a visitor away from Infused Healing.

Practical next step: Correct the links, explain what happens after the form, and give the page a specific title and description.

Impact: HighEffort: LowConfidence: High
2Now
Answers F Local 1, sources S2 and S4 to S7

Clarify local page titles and descriptions

Observation: Several important pages use generic labels or lack a clear short description.

Why it matters: Search results and shared links have less context about the offer and location.

Practical next step: Write a specific title and short description for each core page, add local wording where natural, and connect related pages.

Impact: HighEffort: MediumConfidence: High
3Next
Answers F Conversion 1 and F Content 2, sources S1, S3, and S6

Give each core service a complete visitor path

Observation: The service list is clear, but the most distinctive offers need consistent expectation, fit, location, and next-step detail.

Why it matters: Visitors should not need to start a chat just to understand the basic path.

Practical next step: Refine one page per core offer with who it is for, what to expect, preparation, boundaries, related resources, and one clear action.

Impact: HighEffort: MediumConfidence: High
4Next
Answers F Content 1 and F Content 2, sources S3, S5, S7, and S8

Build an owner-reviewed education system

Observation: The service and shop range is broader than the current article library.

Why it matters: Clear educational context can help visitors understand the offer without overstating what a service or product can do.

Practical next step: Create a small set of reusable articles and posts around self-care routines, sound, herbs, events, and service expectations.

Impact: Medium-HighEffort: MediumConfidence: Medium-High
5Next
Answers F Mobile 1, source S11

Make the mobile first visit lighter

Observation: The homepage check found a heavy load, delayed readiness, unnamed links, and tap spacing issues.

Why it matters: Phone visitors may have to wait or work harder before they can act.

Practical next step: Reduce heavy hero media, limit nonessential widgets by page purpose, correct link labels and tap areas, then recheck mobile pages.

Impact: Medium-HighEffort: MediumConfidence: High
6Later
Answers F Trust 2 and F Content 1, sources S1, S7, S9, and S10

Connect social proof, content, and follow-up

Observation: Social profiles, testimonials, and contact paths exist, but the public content and review picture was limited during this review.

Why it matters: Turning services and events into steady public content can take more owner time than it should.

Practical next step: Set a monthly content rhythm, an owner-approved feedback request, and simple consent-based follow-up for inquiries and events.

Impact: MediumEffort: MediumConfidence: Medium
