Executive Summary

Your current website communicates a calming, trustworthy brand — but a potential client still has to guess whether you offer bookkeeping, accounting support, budgeting, financial consulting, event/community work, or a mix. That is the main business risk: the site asks for trust before it clearly explains what you do.

The strongest opportunity is not about making the site look newer. It is about making the offer easier to understand, giving visitors more confidence to book, and helping search engines send the right people your way. Your existing brand warmth, photography, and community proof are real assets — they just need stronger positioning around them.

Visual Evidence

Major Findings

1. Positioning is too vague for a financial-services buyer

The homepage headline "Find Peace of Mind With Your Finances" is emotionally warm, but it does not answer what you provide, who you serve, where you serve them, or what happens after booking.

  • Clarify whether the core offers are bookkeeping, accounting cleanup, reporting, budgeting, business consultation, or financial organization.
  • State ideal clients: individuals, small businesses, nonprofits, startups — and whether you serve them locally, remotely, or both.
  • Use "peace of mind" as supporting copy after a concrete service promise, not as the primary message.

2. Mobile conversion path is weak

The mobile screenshot shows a very small booking CTA and reduced navigation. The visible "LET'S TALK" text area measured roughly 38 × 7 CSS pixels — far below the recommended 48 × 48 touch-target minimum.

  • Use a real, accessible mobile menu that works with screen readers.
  • Make the primary CTA large enough to tap comfortably on any device.
  • Keep Services, About, Results, and Book/Contact reachable from mobile without excessive scrolling.

3. SEO foundation is thin

Rendered checks found minimal metadata: page titles like HOME/ABOUT/START NOW, Open Graph title/type only, no meta descriptions, no JSON-LD structured data, and no semantic H1/H2 structure on several pages.

  • Add unique titles and meta descriptions for every page.
  • Use one visible H1 per page and logical H2/H3 structure throughout.
  • Add LocalBusiness or ProfessionalService schema using verified business details.
  • Create real service pages — not just generic booking buttons.

4. Trust proof exists but is underused

The About page includes seven years of experience, MBA/accounting background, senior corporate accounting experience, mentoring, and operational/reporting skills. The Start Now page includes testimonials. These are valuable assets — they just need to be packaged into buyer-facing proof.

  • Show "problem → service → outcome" proof blocks rather than listing credentials in isolation.
  • Connect your background to client outcomes: cleaner books, better reporting, less confusion, stronger systems.
  • Add privacy and security reassurance — prospects may share sensitive financial details and need to feel confident doing so.

5. Accessibility and image semantics need work

Images on the reviewed pages mostly lacked useful alt text. Some meaningful photos and the hero image had empty alt attributes, which hurts both accessibility and SEO.

  • Add descriptive alt text to all meaningful images.
  • Mark purely decorative elements as decorative so screen readers ignore them.
  • Check color contrast and focus states during any redesign.

6. Robots / sitemap / favicon access needs verification

Direct checks returned 403 errors for /robots.txt, /sitemap.xml, and /favicon.ico. This may be bot filtering rather than a real block, so it should be verified in a browser or Search Console. If blocked, search engines cannot crawl or index your pages effectively.

What a Better Website Should Say Immediately

  • What: the exact financial services you offer.
  • Who: your audience — individuals, small businesses, nonprofits, or another segment.
  • Outcome: organized finances, clear reporting, better decisions, reduced stress.
  • Proof: your credentials, testimonials, community involvement, and client stories.
  • Next step: what the consultation includes, whether it is free, how long it takes, and what happens after booking.
